# THE MILLER'S DEBT — the notes
### The input side of the demonstration build. This is what a real, messy adventure outline looks like.

> Read this next to `demo.html`. Everything in these notes appears in the tracker unmarked, because
> the author wrote it. Everything the tracker shows in **red** is something Claude added that is

---

PARTY: 4 players, level 3, D&D 2024. About two hours. No magic items to speak of.
Tone: damp English folk horror, but not grim — the village is warm, it just won't talk.

CAMPAIGN NAME: The Miller's Debt

---

## THE SHAPE

```
0:00-0:15   The causeway in. Cold open on the road.
0:15-0:40   Ashfen. Three things to find, they'll get two.
0:40-0:55   FIGHT: the scarecrow field
0:55-1:15   The mill house. The strongbox puzzle.
1:15-1:35   FIGHT: the drowned wheel
1:35-1:55   FIGHT: Old Grist
1:55-2:00   Epilogue. Five minutes. End on Wick.
```

Two chapters: **Ashfen** (the village, and what it won't say) and **The Mill** (everything the
village wouldn't say).

---

## SCENE 1 — THE CAUSEWAY

Cold open on the road, last of the light. The carter won't go past the fen boundary and won't say
why; he waits for his money at the boundary stone.

A scarecrow in the third field has its head turned to watch the road. If a player points at it,
agree with them and move on.

Ask each player what their character is hoping is at the end of this road. **Write the answers
down** — Old Grist uses them later.

They should hear the mill wheel before they see it.

## SCENE 2 — ASHFEN

Sixty people, all polite, none of them will discuss the mill.

- **Sister Halloway** at the chapel will talk. The miller has been gone eleven days and nobody has
  gone to look. She's ashamed of that and says so.
- **Wick**, the miller's boy, nine years old, throwing stones at the water and not going home.
  He *knows more than he is saying*.
- The reeve's man came through nine months ago and again in the spring. Two people mention it and
  both change the subject.

Halloway comes with them to the mill. Wick follows whether they let him or not.

## SCENE 3 — FIGHT: THE SCARECROW FIELD

The only route to the mill crosses the barley. Four scarecrows, standing where scarecrows stand.
**They don't move until the party does something.** Let the players start this fight.

Fire is the obvious answer and everything is soaked. Let them try it, let it half-work.

There's *something in the reeds that hunts in twos* — comes out on round two, from behind.

Ditches of standing water across the field. Anyone who ends a turn in one has started the Damp.

## SCENE 4 — THE MILL HOUSE

Tidy. Bed made. Eleven days of dust and one clean patch on the table where something was taken.

**The strongbox** is under the floor by the grain hopper. Four brass dials — a wave, a wheel, a
sheaf of wheat, a moon — and a rhyme scratched into the lid:

> What comes first has no hands.
> What comes next is turned by it.
> What comes third is broken by that.
> And what comes last is my only witness.

Answer: water, wheel, wheat, moon. He milled at night, which is the thing they haven't worked out.
Wrong order sticks a needle in someone — 1d4 and poisoned a minute, never a lockout.

Inside: 31 gold, the deed, and the tax letter. **The letter is the why** — he couldn't pay.

The wheel is turning and nothing is driving it. Nobody has opened the sluice in eleven days.

## SCENE 5 — FIGHT: THE DROWNED WHEEL

*The pond ones come out.* Two of them. Plus the reed things again.

Halloway's Turn Undead is her moment here.

## SCENE 6 — FIGHT: OLD GRIST

The boss. She's in the millrace under the wheel and doesn't surface until her first turn.

She **regenerates while she's in running water**. The sluice lever is on the north wall; closing it
switches that off for good. That's the fight.

She says one true thing about a character in their parent's voice — use the scene 1 answers.

Once per fight she opens the sluice and floods the whole floor.

The miller is at the bottom of the race, still turning the wheel, and he is not going to stop.

Two scarecrows come down the ladder on round two — a clock, not a threat.

If it's going badly, Halloway steps into the water. That's the out, and it costs her.

## SCENE 7 — EPILOGUE

Five minutes. The wheel stops for the first time in eleven days and the whole valley notices the
silence. Nobody apologises. Somebody has already brought bread.

Wick asks whether his father waved. **Do not answer that yourself** — let a player.

## THE CREATURES

**Bog Scarecrow** — CR 1/2 construct. AC 13, HP 27 (5d8+5), speed 30. Con save +3.
STR 14 DEX 12 CON 13 INT 4 WIS 10 CHA 5.
Immune poison, **vulnerable fire**. Indistinguishable from a real scarecrow while still.
Two claws, +4, 1d6+2. Terrifying Glare: 30 ft, DC 11 Wis or Frightened one round.
AT START OF COMBAT: they aren't moving. Nothing happens until the party touches the fence,
crosses a ditch, or attacks.

**Drowned Miller** — CR 2 undead. AC 12, HP 45 (7d8+14), speed 25, swim 30. Con save +4.
STR 16 DEX 12 CON 15 INT 8 WIS 10 CHA 6.
Two slams, +5, 1d8+3 bludgeoning + 1d4 cold. Both slams on one target = DC 13 Str or grappled and
dragged 10 ft toward the water. Casts Ray of Frost and Fog Cloud, DC 11, two 1st-level slots.

**Old Grist** — CR 5 fey hag. AC 16, HP 95 (10d10+40), speed 30, swim 40. Con save +7, Wis +5.
STR 18 DEX 14 CON 18 INT 13 WIS 15 CHA 12.
Two legendary resistances, three legendary actions.
Grind +7, reach 10, 2d8+4 and grappled (DC 15) — she holds them *under*.
Fen Chill +7, 2d6+4 cold and −10 ft speed.
Legendary: Wade · Sluice (2) · Whisper.
Spells DC 14: Ray of Frost, Fog Cloud, Hold Person, Misty Step 2/day.
AT START OF ITS TURN: regains 8 HP while any part of her is in running water.

**Wick** — NPC, nine years old. Not a combatant, hides if a fight starts. Knows the mill, knows
where the sluice lever is, knows his father worked nights. Will not leave while his father is
down there.

**Sister Halloway** — NPC, village cleric, CR 2-ish. AC 16, HP 33. Sacred Flame, Cure Wounds,
Bless, Lesser Restoration, Spiritual Weapon. Turn Undead once. She will run out and then she's a
woman with a mace.

*(Something in the reeds — I haven't statted it. Two of them, they hunt in pairs, they can swim.
Whatever you think is right for level 3.)*

## THE FIGHTS

**Fight 1** — 4 scarecrows, 2 of the reed things. The drowned barley at dusk.
MAP: flat field, two ditches of standing water across it, three rows of fence posts, churned mud.
Party comes in through the gate at the bottom right.

**Fight 2** — 2 drowned millers, 2 reed things. *The mill yard, the pond on one side, a cart to
hide behind.*

**Fight 3** — Old Grist plus 2 scarecrows. Inside the wheelhouse.
MAP: the millrace runs across the middle, four squares deep, wheel at the left end. Two short
stairs cross it. Sluice lever on a plinth top left. Sacks and broken gearing for cover along the
bottom.

## THE HOMEBREW RULE — THE DAMP

Ashfen water doesn't want to let go of you. Constitution save when you get out, and again every
10 minutes you stay in. Tier is total time in the water tonight.

```
Up to 10 min   DC 10   fail: 1 exhaustion                              success: nothing
10-30 min      DC 13   fail: 1 exhaustion + roll What the Water Says   success: 1 exhaustion
Over 30 min    DC 16   fail: 2 exhaustion, speed halved till long rest success: 1 exhaustion
Held under     —       no save, that's drowning, see Old Grist
```

What the Water Says (d6):
1. You hear your own name, from under the surface, in your own voice.
2. Everything you're carrying is suddenly, impossibly heavy.
3. You're certain the water is warmer further down.
4. For a moment you can see clearly underwater, and there is somebody standing on the bottom.
5. Your lantern gutters and burns green. No warmth for an hour.
6. You are dry. Your clothes are dry, your hair is dry, and nobody else can see it.

Getting dry by a fire clears the count. It doesn't clear exhaustion.
